How to Improve your Local Vision Language Model

TLDR: the best way to boost a VLM's accuracy is by using another VLM from a different model family to judge the original model's answers.

What's the best way to boost the reliability of your vision language model? Average the outputs over multiple calls? use another VLM judge to verify the output of the original model? Give the model a zoom tool to better inspect the image?

If you want to just have the best performance possible: no problem, just pay the $$ for a frontier model. Those costs can add up quickly though. On top of that, if you care about retaining your own data, then you don't want to let another company see your data. So that leaves us with open source, local models. In particular, I'm most interested in small, efficient models that can easily fit on a single consumer GPU (1B - 12B parameters)

Despite how frequently VLM judges are used in research and production, there is surprisingly little research studying how to make them as effective as possible. I am a fan of Lu et al's paper "When Does Verification Pay Off? A Closer Look at LLMs as Solution Verifiers"https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02304. The main finding of this paper is that the best LLM judges are from a different family than the model that generated the orignial answer. So for example, if you used Qwen to generate an answer, you should NOT use another Qwen model to verify the answer. I was curious about whether or not this finding would apply to VLM so I spent over 300 GPU hours my local 3090's to generate these results

Methodology

I ran all evaluations on CharXiv, a benchmark where the model has to reason about scientific figures to answer questions like "At Epoch 60, which training method has a higher Adversarial Accuracy?".

I tested 13 open source VLMs across 4 families. Qwen3-VL at 2B, 4B and 8B. Gemma at E2B, E4B and 12B. InternVL3.5 at 1B, 2B, 4B, 8B and 14B. LLaVA-1.5 at 7B and 13B. Each model was run locally through vLLM on a single RTX 3090.

For each question, a solver model reads the chart and the question and writes an answer to the benchmark's question. Then, a VLM judge then reads the same chart, the question and the solver's answer, and decides whether to accept or reject it. Every one of the 13 model plays both roles, so I get a full 13×13 grid of solver and verifier pairings.

Following Lu et al, I run the VLM judge and the generation model in a loop with the where if the judge rejects an answer as incorrect, the generation model generates a fresh response to the question to try gain (capped at 5 retries). I measure the VLM judge under three different settings:

  1. Self is a model judging its own answers.
  2. Intra is a judge from the same family at a different size.
  3. Cross is a judge from a different family.

As a comparison, I also try two other test-time compute methods:

  1. Majority vote draws 5 independent samples and picks the most common answer.
  2. Zoom tool gives the solver a tool to crop and magnify the image, and is run with different maximum allowed budgets of 2, 4 and 8 total crops.

Results

Gemma is unsurprisingly the most accuarate with Qwen and InternVL closely behind. I included Llava as a sanity baseline and because it is one of my favorite VLMs. Its accuracy is... not great.

Majority Vote

Taking the average answer over 5 runs increases accuracy slightly, but is not really worth it given that you have to run 5 independent runs every time you want answer.

Zoom Tool

The "Thinking with Images" report from the o3 announcment where they showed how training a model to use a cropping tool to zoom in on parts of the image gives huge boosts in accuracy is one of my favorite technical reports of all time. This cropping ability is now trained by default into all the frontier models. However, there's surprisingly little information out there about how to run open source models with the same tool and how it affects accuracy. I think the reason why the open source labs don't like to highlight this ability while the OpenAI, Anthropic, etc all love to show off their benchmarks numbers (*with tools) is that the small models aren't good at consistent tool use. I saw small +2% gains with Qwen and +4% gains with Gemma*. InternVL consisently messes up the zoom calls and repeatedly zooms into irrelevant parts of the image and confuses itself. Llava was not trained to have multi-turn conversations with multiple images, so I did not test the zoom tool calls with it.

VLM Judge

By far, the best increase to accuracy was by using a VLM judge in combination with the orginial solver model. I let the VLM judge determine if the solver's reponse was correct or incorrect. If the judge determined that the original model's answer was incorrect, it would restart a fresh instance of the solver model to try again.
